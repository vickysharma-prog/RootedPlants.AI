# Rewards and the money

## Why rewards at all

People act on rewards. Every loyalty scheme, every credit card points
programme, every streak counter runs on it, and we have aimed all of it at
spending. Rooted aims it at the thing our lives actually depend on.

That is the pitch in one line, and it is also the whole product logic. The
reward is not a marketing layer on top of a plant care app. It is the reason
the care happens.

## How points are earned

Points attach to **verified tasks**, one at a time.

| Event | Points |
|---|---|
| Verified care task | base |
| Streak, task after task without a miss | rising multiplier |
| Milestone, plant alive and cared for at 30, 90, 180, 365 days | large one-off |
| Register a new plant | small, one-off |
| Replant after a loss | bonus |

The streak is what makes this compound. A single watering is worth little. Four
months of not missing is worth a lot. That shape rewards exactly the behaviour
the product exists to create, and it is also what makes the data valuable,
because an unbroken series is worth more than scattered points.

## When things go wrong

A plant can die for reasons that have nothing to do with the person caring for
it. Disease, animals, weather, somebody cuts it down.

**Loss is reported, not penalised.** The user reports it with a photo, the
points already earned stay earned, and the streak carries over to their next
plant. Replanting earns a bonus on top.

The system compounds on what someone has already built rather than resetting
it. Somebody who has cared for a tree for six months and then loses it is the
most likely person in the world to plant another one, and the point balance
they keep is what makes that the obvious next step.

## What points turn into

Three kinds, and they are not all coupons.

**Partner rewards.** Discount codes and offers from partners. For a partner
this is customer acquisition they already pay for, with a better story attached
to it.

**Recognition.** A public profile page for each plant, showing the species, the
photo history and how long it has been alive. Milestone certificates at 90 and
365 days. These cost close to nothing and they are a real pull, because the
whole behaviour the product is built on started with somebody wanting to post a
photo of a tree.

**Standing.** Streaks, totals, and how much time a person has kept alive.

Starting with all three solves the cold start. Recognition works from day one
with no partner signed, so the loop is never empty while the catalogue fills.

## Where the money comes from

Three sources, and the first is the largest.

### 1. CSR, government and NGO programmes

Companies, state programmes and NGOs spend enormous amounts on tree planting
drives every year. What they get back is a photograph from planting day. What
happened to the saplings afterwards is not recorded anywhere.

Rooted is the record. A drive run through Rooted produces a verified, ongoing
answer to the only question that matters, which is how many are still alive and
in what condition. That is the thing being bought, and it is the same thing
that funds the rewards.

### 2. Partner-funded rewards

Partners fund their own offers, because for them it is acquisition spend.
Nurseries, garden supply and local businesses go first: low acquisition cost
and an audience that matches exactly.

### 3. Advertising

An app people open every day, in a category advertisers want to be near, is an
ad surface. It is not the first source but it is a real one, and it scales with
the same daily habit the product is already built to create.

## The asset

Rewards attract users. Users produce something that does not exist anywhere
else: geotagged, time-stamped, species-labelled, photo-verified survival data,
at scale, over years.

Nobody has that. It is what the CSR and government money is actually buying,
and it is an Earth Forward argument on its own, because survival rates that
nobody measures cannot be improved.

## For the hackathon

The catalogue is seeded with demo partners. Generic names, no real brands, no
real logos. This is what a rewards catalogue looks like before partners sign,
and what it needs to demonstrate is the loop.

Recognition rewards, the plant profile page and the certificate, are worth
building for the demo precisely because they work without a partner and they
show well on camera.
